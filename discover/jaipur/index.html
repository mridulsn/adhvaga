Rajasthan

Jaipur

Photo: Chainwit., CC BY-SA 4.0

Jaipur, the capital of Rajasthan, was founded in 1727 by Sawai Jai Singh II, who moved his court down from the hill fort of Amer. Its planned walled city, painted pink, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, with the City Palace, Jantar Mantar and Hawa Mahal at its heart and bazaars along its broad streets. Forts at Amer, Jaigarh and Nahargarh guard the hills to the north.

Know before you go

The basics, each with the place it came from. Checked 2026-11-10.

Getting there

  • Jaipur International Airport is at Sanganer, about 13 km from the city (Wikipedia). It has domestic flights from Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and other cities, and international flights to Dubai, Muscat, Singapore and Bangkok (Rajasthan Tourism). (tourism.rajasthan.gov.in)
  • Jaipur Junction, the busiest station in Rajasthan, has trains from Delhi, Agra, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Jodhpur, Udaipur and other cities. (en.wikipedia.org)
  • Rajasthan State Road Transport Corporation (RSRTC) runs AC and non-AC buses from Delhi and across Rajasthan; the main bus stand is Sindhi Camp (Wikivoyage, undated). (en.wikivoyage.org)
  • Jaipur is on National Highway 48 between Delhi and Mumbai; NH 21 links it with Agra and NH 52 with Kota. Wikipedia gives the distance from New Delhi as 268 km. (en.wikipedia.org)

Hot semi-arid climate with long, very hot summers and short, mild winters. December to February average below 20 C and are typically dry and pleasant; annual rain is over 625 mm, mostly in July and August. (en.wikipedia.org)

Good to know

  • State-run monuments (Amer, Hawa Mahal, Jantar Mantar, Nahargarh, Albert Hall, Sisodia Rani Garden) are closed on Dhulandi, and entry is free on 30 March (Rajasthan Diwas), 18 April (World Heritage Day), 18 May (World Museum Day) and 27 September (World Tourism Day). Children below 7 enter free. (obms-tourist.rajasthan.gov.in)
  • A composite ticket (Indian Rs 400, foreigner Rs 1000 on the official portal; students Rs 100 / Rs 500) is sold for the state monuments. News reports said rates would roughly double from 1 January 2026, but the portal still showed these figures on 2026-11-10, so check when booking. (obms-tourist.rajasthan.gov.in)
  • City Palace is privately run and is not on the composite ticket; its own composite covers City Palace, Jaigarh and the Royal Cenotaphs. (citypalace.org)
  • The markets (Johari Bazaar, Bapu Bazaar, Nehru Bazaar, MI Road) are generally closed on Sundays. Rajasthali, the Rajasthan government showroom, is on MI Road. (tourism.rajasthan.gov.in)
  • The Jaipur Metro Pink Line runs from Mansarovar to Badi Chaupar in the walled city; fares Rs 5-25 (Wikivoyage, undated). (en.wikivoyage.org)
  • Wikivoyage warns of gem scams and of auto-rickshaw drivers steering visitors to commission shops; agree an all-in price for a day hire. (en.wikivoyage.org)

Amer, on the hill above Maota Lake, north of the city

The hilltop palace-fort of the Kachhwaha rulers, begun by Raja Man Singh I in 1592, and their capital until the court moved to Jaipur in 1727. Built of red sandstone and white marble in a mix of Hindu and Mughal styles, it overlooks Maota Lake and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Suggested visit
Suggested visit 2 h - 3 h 30 min ~ Estimated
Entry
General ticket: Indian Rs 100 (student Rs 20), foreigner Rs 500 (student Rs 150). Night ticket at the same rates. Composite ticket: Indian Rs 400 (student Rs 100), foreigner Rs 1000 (student Rs 500). Children below 7 free. Official portal, undated; rates may have risen in 2026. (obms-tourist.rajasthan.gov.in)

Cheel ka Teela ridge above Amer Fort

An 18th-century hill fort built by Sawai Jai Singh II on the ridge above Amer Fort and Maota Lake. It still keeps the look of an imposing citadel, and Rajasthan Tourism says visitors can see Jaivana, the world's largest cannon, there.

Suggested visit
Suggested visit 1 h 30 min - 2 h 30 min ~ Estimated
Entry
No standalone rate found on an official page. The City Palace Museum composite ticket covers City Palace, Jaigarh and the Royal Cenotaphs: Indian adult Rs 500 (student/child under 12, senior or defence Rs 300); international adult Rs 1700 (youth Rs 850). (citypalace.org)

Ridge of the Aravalli hills above the old city

A fort built by Sawai Jai Singh II in 1734 on the ridge that forms the northern backdrop of Jaipur, later expanded in 1868. Inside is Madhavendra Bhawan, a palace of matching suites for the queens joined by painted corridors; the walls give wide views over the city, especially at dusk.

Suggested visit

Entry

Badi Chaupar, Sireh Deori Bazaar, old walled city

Jaipur's best-known landmark, built in 1799 by Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh so the women of the court could watch street life unseen. The five-storey pink sandstone facade is a honeycomb of small latticed windows; visitors can climb to the top for views, and the courtyard holds a small archaeological museum.

Suggested visit

Centre of the walled city, entrances near Jantar Mantar and at Jaleb Chowk

The palace complex built by Sawai Jai Singh II, founder of Jaipur, mixing Rajput and Mughal architecture. Its courtyards and galleries (Mubarak Mahal, Sileh Khana, Sabha Niwas and others) form the City Palace Museum, while the seven-storey Chandra Mahal is still the royal family's residence.

Suggested visit

Museum: Indian adult Rs 400 (student/child under 12 Rs 200; senior or defence Rs 200); international adult Rs 1200 (youth Rs 600). Composite with Jaigarh and Royal Cenotaphs: Rs 500 Indian / Rs 1700 international. Chandra Mahal Royal Tour: Rs 4000 Indian / Rs 5000 international adult. (citypalace.org)

Next to City Palace, old walled city

The largest of the five astronomical observatories built by Sawai Jai Singh II, completed in 1734 and now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Its masonry instruments, including the world's largest stone sundial, measure time and track the sun, moon and planets; an interpretation centre explains how they work.

Man Sagar Lake promenade, on the Jaipur-Amer road

A five-storey sandstone palace in the middle of Man Sagar Lake, first built around 1699 as a duck-hunting lodge and enlarged in the 18th century. When the lake is full the lower floors are under water, so it seems to float; it is viewed from the lakeside promenade on the Amer road.

Ram Niwas Garden, opposite New Gate

Rajasthan's oldest museum, in an Indo-Saracenic building designed by Samuel Swinton Jacob (foundation stone 1876, opened 1887). Collections include metalwork, carpets, arms, sculpture, miniature paintings, a Persian garden carpet and an Egyptian mummy.

Suggested visit
Suggested visit 1-2 h ~ Estimated
Entry
General ticket: Indian Rs 50 (student Rs 20), foreigner Rs 300 (student Rs 150). Children below 7 free. Official portal, undated; rates may have risen in 2026. (obms-tourist.rajasthan.gov.in)

Tilak Nagar, at the foot of Moti Dungri hill

A white-marble temple to Vishnu (Narayan) and Lakshmi, built by the Birla family and opened in 1988. Its carvings cover mythological themes, its three domes are said to represent three faiths, and it is lit up at night; a small museum shows Birla family items.

Gorge in the hills east of the city, off the Jaipur-Agra road

An old pilgrimage site of temples and pavilions built into a narrow gorge, where a natural spring fills a series of sacred kunds (tanks) used for bathing. It is known as the Monkey Temple for its many resident macaques, and a path climbs to the Surya (Sun) temple on the ridge with views over Jaipur.

Jawahar Circle, Malviya Nagar, near the airport

A decorated gateway at Jawahar Circle, a large circular park built by the Jaipur Development Authority with jogging tracks, rose gardens and a musical fountain. The gate may look like a Rajput-era structure, but it was built in 2016.

Jai Niwas garden, inside the City Palace complex

A Radha-Krishna temple in the City Palace complex whose deity Sawai Jai Singh II brought from Vrindavan in the 18th century. The deity of this rare spire-less temple was worshipped by the former royal family and is revered by local people.

Old walled city, between Sanganeri Gate and Badi Chaupar

One of the main bazaars of the walled city, best known for jewellery: hundreds of shops sell jewellery of all kinds, gems and stones. Rajasthan Tourism lists it among Jaipur's famous shopping streets.

Ghat ki Guni, on the Jaipur-Agra road

A terraced Mughal-style garden built in 1710 by Sawai Jai Singh II for his Sisodia queen from Udaipur. It has fountains, water channels, painted pavilions and a small palace with murals of Radha and Krishna; peacocks are often seen in the grounds.

An old stepwell in Amer town, a short walk from the Anokhi Museum and Amer Fort. Wikivoyage says people come here to fetch water, swim or simply spend time.

A long-running vegetarian restaurant and sweet shop on Johari Bazaar, popular with visitors for traditional sweets such as paneer ghewar, sweet lassi and snacks like samosa, chaat and aloo tikki.

Abhaneri village, Dausa district, off the Jaipur-Agra highway

One of the deepest and largest stepwells in India, dated to the 8th-9th century, with about 3,500 steps in 13 levels cascading to a tank about 30 m below ground. Beside it are the ruins of the Harshat Mata temple.

Suggested visit
Suggested visit 1-2 h ~ Estimated

Sambhar, on the Jaipur-Nagaur district border

India's largest inland salt lake, a seasonal salt flat that supplies much of the country's salt and a Ramsar wetland where tens of thousands of flamingos winter. Rajasthan Tourism recommends sunset views from the Shakambhari Mata temple and the salt works' own small railway.
