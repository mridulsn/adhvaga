Himachal Pradesh - about 2,206 m

Shimla

Photo: Slyronit, CC BY-SA 4.0

Shimla, the capital of Himachal Pradesh, is a hill town spread along a ridge with seven spurs in the south-western Himalayas. The British made it the summer capital of British India in 1864, and colonial buildings such as Christ Church, the Gaiety Theatre and the Viceregal Lodge still stand. Life centres on The Ridge, a large open space with views of the snow ranges, and the Mall Road just below it, a car-free shopping street. Jakhu Hill, crowned by a Hanuman temple, rises above the town, and the narrow-gauge Kalka-Shimla Railway, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, climbs up from the plains.

Know before you go

The basics, each with the place it came from. Checked 2026-11-10.

Getting there

  • Shimla Airport at Jubbarhatti is 23 km from the city, with flights from Delhi (Himachal Tourism). The district site says it has limited operations depending on season and weather, and that Chandigarh Airport, about 120 km away, is the main alternative. (himachaltourism.gov.in)
  • Trains from Delhi run to Kalka, where the narrow-gauge Kalka-Shimla line (96 km, about 6 hours) climbs to Shimla. As of 27 October 2026 the toy train was reported fully suspended after monsoon damage in Solan district (see the Kalka-Shimla Railway entry), so check before planning on it. (hpshimla.nic.in)
  • Ordinary and luxury coaches run regularly from Delhi and Chandigarh (Himachal Tourism). Out-of-town buses use the ISBT at Tutikandi, about 4 km from the main town, which is linked by local buses and taxis (district site). The district site says buses run from Chandigarh about every half hour. (hpshimla.nic.in)
  • By road, Shimla is about 119 km from Chandigarh (about 3.5 hours by car) and 370 km from Delhi (about 8 hours by car), per the district road table. Roads from the Cart Road up to the Mall side are sealed and restricted; private cars need a paid permit to use them. (hpshimla.nic.in)

When to go

Himachal Tourism calls Shimla a year-round destination: December to February for snowfall, April to June for cool summer weather, and March and September to November for spring and autumn. July and August bring the rains and rising mists. (himachaltourism.gov.in)

Subtropical highland climate. Summer averages are about 16-25 C and winter about 3-11 C. August is the wettest month (about 350 mm) and November the driest (about 14 mm); annual rainfall is about 1,487 mm. Snowfall, once usual in December, now tends to come in January or early February. Himachal Tourism advises heavy woollens from November to March. (en.wikipedia.org)

Good to know

  • Shimla sits on a hilltop: the Mall, Ridge, Kali Bari and Lakkar Bazaar are reached on foot from the ring road. An HP Tourism lift links the Cart Road to the Mall. (hpshimla.nic.in)
  • Roads leading from the Cart Road to the Mall side are sealed and restricted, and parking along the Cart Road is prohibited. Private vehicles can get a temporary permit (Rs 100 per day) from the Deputy Commissioner's office near the CTO on the Mall. Main parking: ISBT, near the Lift (about 800 cars), near the High Court, Railway Station godown, Old Bus Stand, Chhota Shimla and Sanjauli. (hpshimla.nic.in)
  • Local buses run about 7 AM to 9 PM, mainly on the ring road. HPTDC runs daily sightseeing buses that can be booked at the Tourist Information Centre on the Mall. (hpshimla.nic.in)
  • Book rooms ahead in the peak summer and Christmas seasons (district site). (hpshimla.nic.in)
  • An open-air ice-skating rink below The Ridge on the northern slope operates from about November to February, with a carnival around Christmas and New Year. A Summer Festival is held in May or June. (himachaltourism.gov.in)
  • Tourist Information Centre, Shimla: 0177-2654589, 2832498. HPTDC marketing office: 0177-2652561 / 2658302. (himachaltourism.gov.in)

Town centre, above the Mall Road, between Scandal Point (west) and Lakkar Bazaar (east)

The large open space at the heart of Shimla and the town's cultural hub. On a clear day there are views of the snow peaks to the north. Christ Church, the Tudor-style State Library, the Town Hall, the slate roofline of the Gaiety Theatre and statues including Mahatma Gandhi's stand around it. Underneath lies a water reservoir built in the 1880s that still supplies much of the town.

Runs below and parallel to The Ridge; joins it at Scandal Point

Shimla's main street, built in the colonial era a level below The Ridge. It is lined with showrooms, department stores, restaurants and cafes, and the fronts of the Gaiety Theatre and Town Hall face it. Vehicles other than emergency vehicles are not allowed. The Himachal Emporium at the west end sells local handicrafts and handloom, and the old Indian Coffee House stands a stone's throw from Scandal Point.

East end of The Ridge, next to the State Library

A neo-Gothic Anglican church that is Shimla's best-known landmark and visible for kilometres around. Designed by Colonel J. T. Boileau, its foundation stone was laid in 1844 and it was consecrated in 1857, making it the second-oldest church in North India. The clock was added in 1860, the porch in 1873 and a British pipe organ in 1899. It is lit up at night, and services are held in Hindi and English.

Top of Jakhu Hill, above The Ridge

An ancient temple to Lord Hanuman on Jakhu Hill, the highest point in Shimla. A 108-foot statue of Hanuman, unveiled in 2010, towers over the hilltop. Most people walk up from The Ridge (about 2 km, a steep 30-45 minutes); taxis, shared HRTC taxis and ponies are also available. The Jakhu Ropeway, opened in 2017 by Jagson International, rides from near the Ridge to the temple in about 6 minutes.

Observatory Hill, Boileauganj, at the western end of town

The former summer residence of the British Viceroys, a grand Scottish-castle-style building designed by Henry Irwin and finished in 1888 under Lord Dufferin. It was one of the first buildings in India with electric lighting. The 1945 Shimla Conference and the 1946 Cabinet Mission talks that shaped Indian independence were held here. Since 1965 it has housed the Indian Institute of Advanced Study; parts of the building and the lawns are open to visitors on guided tours, and a Tagore Gallery opened in March 2026.

West end of The Ridge, where it meets the Mall Road

The junction where the Mall Road meets The Ridge on its west side; Himachal Tourism calls it a good place to catch up on the day's news and gossip. A statue of the freedom fighter Lala Lajpat Rai stands here, and the General Post Office is next door. The name is said to come from a story that a Maharaja of Patiala eloped with a British lady, after which he was banned from Shimla and built his own summer retreat at Chail.

Mall Road, below The Ridge, beside the Town Hall

A Victorian Gothic Revival theatre designed by Henry Irwin and opened on 30 May 1887 as part of the old town-hall complex. In the Raj era Viceroy Lord Lytton wrote and staged a play here and Rudyard Kipling acted on its stage; later many well-known Indian actors and singers performed here. Renovated and now known as the Gaiety Heritage Cultural Complex, it is still the hub of the state's cultural events.

A flat valley floor below the town, north-west of The Ridge

The largest stretch of level ground in Shimla, ringed by deodar forest and reached by a sharp descent. In the Raj era it was the town's playground for fairs, horse races, polo and cricket, and the Durand Football Tournament was first held here in 1888. Today it is mainly a helipad and army area, with a golf course and an Army Heritage Museum.

Top of Inverarm Hill, Chaura Maidan, about 2.5 km west of Scandal Point

The state museum, opened in 1974 in Inverarm, a Victorian house that was once a British officials' summer residence. Its collection covers sculpture, bronzes, coins, jewellery, manuscripts, weapons, stamps and a fine set of Pahari and other Indian miniature paintings, and it has a library. The hilltop garden is good for a short stroll.

Bantony Hill, a short walk from the Mall Road

A temple to the goddess Kali in her form as Shyamala, from whom the name Shimla may come. It was founded by Bengalis in the early days of British Shimla and remains a much-visited shrine for the town's Hindus and the Bengali community. The temple grounds look out over the Old Bus Stand, Annandale and the railway station.

West of town, about 1 km beyond Boileauganj, on the Kalka-Shimla line

One of Shimla's seven hills, a quiet suburb of pine and deodar with shady walks and its own station on the Kalka-Shimla Railway. Mahatma Gandhi stayed here in the Georgian house of Rajkumari Amrit Kaur on his visits to Shimla. Himachal Pradesh University has its campus here, and Chadwick Falls is within walking distance.

Hilltop south of Shimla, reached by a road up from Shoghi on the Shimla-Kalka highway

A hilltop temple to the goddess Tara, a form of Durga, set among deodar and pine forest with 360-degree views over the hills. Wikipedia traces it to Sen dynasty kings around 1766; the present wood-and-stone temple with a slate roof in traditional hill style was rebuilt in 2018. On auspicious days devotees host a free community lunch. You can drive up from Shoghi or walk up from Tara Devi station on the toy-train line.

East of Shimla on NH 5 (the old Hindustan-Tibet road), towards Fagu

A small resort at about 2,720 m east of Shimla and the highest point in the surrounding area. In winter a path through the potato fields becomes a popular ski track. The Himalayan Nature Park here keeps more than 180 species of Himalayan birds and animals, and Kufri Fun World, at about 2,800 m beside the highway, claims the highest go-kart track. Mahasu Peak is the high point nearby.

North-east of Shimla on the old Hindustan-Tibet road

A town at about 2,146 m that became a weekend retreat for Shimla's British elite in the late 19th century. It is much less commercialised than Shimla. The President of India's summer retreat is at Chharabra nearby, as is Wildflower Hall, once Lord Kitchener's residence and now a luxury hotel. Craignano Nature Park, on a branch road for Mashobra, has a tulip garden that blooms around March-April.

North of Shimla, beyond Mashobra

A wide meadow at about 2,200 m ringed by dark deodar forest, with an 18-hole golf course. Lord Curzon, Viceroy from 1898 to 1905, liked it so much that he named his daughter after it. A nature walk climbs uphill through the woods. Local legend says the glade has no stones or trees because of a battle between two local gods, and the place is named after the deity Nal Deo.

Solan district, south-east of Shimla

A hill station in thick cedar forest, built up by the Maharajas of Patiala as their summer retreat. The Chail Palace dates from 1891-92 (rebuilt in 1951), and the Patiala rulers also built the Chail cricket ground. Other sights are Kali Tibba and a Hanuman temple, and the hills are popular for treks and camping.

Shimla railway station on the Cart Road; the line runs 96 km down to Kalka

A narrow-gauge (2 ft 6 in) mountain railway built between 1898 and 1903 to link the summer capital to the plains. It climbs from about 656 m at Kalka to 2,075 m at Shimla through 102 working tunnels (the longest, at Barog, is about 1.1 km), 969 bridges and 912 curves. Since 2008 it has been part of the Mountain Railways of India UNESCO World Heritage Site. Trains on the line include the Himalayan Queen, the Shivalik Express, a glass-roofed Vistadome and a small rail motor car.

Suggested visit
