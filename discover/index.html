Uttarakhand

Mussoorie

Mussoorie is a British-era hill station on a ridge above Dehradun in the Garhwal Himalayan foothills, often called the Queen of the Hills. Its Mall Road, viewpoints and walks look north to snow peaks and south over the Doon Valley. Next to it is Landour, a quieter old cantonment town.

Uttarakhand

Nainital

Nainital is a British-era hill station in the Kumaon Himalayas, built around the crescent-shaped Naini Lake and ringed by seven hills. Founded in the 1840s, it was the summer capital of the United Provinces and is still home to the Governor's summer residence and the Uttarakhand High Court. The Mall Road runs along the lake between Mallital at the north end and Tallital at the south, and viewpoints such as Snow View and Naina Peak look out to the snow ranges.

Uttarakhand

Rishikesh

Rishikesh is a pilgrimage town on the Ganga where the river leaves the Himalayan foothills, known as the Yoga Capital of the World. Ashrams and temples line both banks, from Triveni Ghat in the old town up to Swargashram and Lakshman Jhula. It is also a major base for white-water rafting and a gateway to the Char Dham shrines.

Himachal Pradesh

Shimla

Shimla, the capital of Himachal Pradesh, is a hill town spread along a ridge with seven spurs in the south-western Himalayas. The British made it the summer capital of British India in 1864, and colonial buildings such as Christ Church, the Gaiety Theatre and the Viceregal Lodge still stand. Life centres on The Ridge, a large open space with views of the snow ranges, and the Mall Road just below it, a car-free shopping street. Jakhu Hill, crowned by a Hanuman temple, rises above the town, and the narrow-gauge Kalka-Shimla Railway, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, climbs up from the plains.

Rajasthan

Jaipur

Jaipur, the capital of Rajasthan, was founded in 1727 by Sawai Jai Singh II, who moved his court down from the hill fort of Amer. Its planned walled city, painted pink, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, with the City Palace, Jantar Mantar and Hawa Mahal at its heart and bazaars along its broad streets. Forts at Amer, Jaigarh and Nahargarh guard the hills to the north.
