Uttarakhand - about 2,000 m

Nainital

Photo: Praveen Singh Bisht, CC BY 2.0

Nainital is a British-era hill station in the Kumaon Himalayas, built around the crescent-shaped Naini Lake and ringed by seven hills. Founded in the 1840s, it was the summer capital of the United Provinces and is still home to the Governor's summer residence and the Uttarakhand High Court. The Mall Road runs along the lake between Mallital at the north end and Tallital at the south, and viewpoints such as Snow View and Naina Peak look out to the snow ranges.

Know before you go

The basics, each with the place it came from. Checked 2026-11-10.

Getting there

  • Pantnagar Airport is the nearest, about 70 km away by road (Uttarakhand Tourism; Wikipedia says about 71 km). Delhi IGI Airport is about 300 km away. (uttarakhandtourism.gov.in)
  • The nearest railhead is Kathgodam, linked to most major cities; the Ranikhet Express runs from New Delhi. Uttarakhand Tourism puts it 23 km from Nainital, while the district site gives 34-38 km depending on the spot in town. Buses and taxis run up from the station. (uttarakhandtourism.gov.in)
  • Buses run from Delhi's Kashmiri Gate ISBT to Nainital in about 7 hours (Uttarakhand Tourism). The Tallital bus station sits on the lake bridge at the south end (district site). (uttarakhandtourism.gov.in)
  • Nainital is on National Highway 109 (formerly NH 87), 40 km from Haldwani, and can also be reached by State Highway 13 from Bajpur, 60 km away. (en.wikipedia.org)

When to go

Uttarakhand Tourism calls Nainital an all-year destination, with the best weather between March and June. Heavy winter snowfall makes it a popular Christmas and New Year destination. (uttarakhandtourism.gov.in)

Subtropical highland climate. July is the wettest month (about 725 mm) and November the driest; the monsoon runs roughly mid-June to mid-October. Winter lasts from mid-November to mid-March, January lows average about 1.7 C, frost and mist are common in December-January, and snow falls on the higher peaks. (en.wikipedia.org)

Good to know

  • In May and June the Mall Road is closed to vehicles in the evening hours so people can stroll. Thandi Road, on the other side of the lake, is closed to vehicles. (nainital.nic.in)
  • Rowing and paddle boats are available at both ends of the lake; the rates are displayed at prepaid booths along the lakeside. (nainital.nic.in)
  • The Tallital lake bridge has the bus station, taxi stand, a railway reservation counter and a post office. (nainital.nic.in)
  • A zoo shuttle service runs from the Nainital bus station. The zoo is closed on Mondays, national holidays, Holi and Diwali. (nainital.nic.in)
  • The ropeway (Mallital to Snow View) runs from 10 AM; the fare is for a return trip, and Uttarakhand Tourism suggests about an hour at the top. (uttarakhandtourism.gov.in)
  • Ponies or horses can be hired to Naina Peak from Snow View or from Barapathar. (nainital.nic.in)

Centre of town, between Mallital (north) and Tallital (south)

The crescent-shaped lake at the heart of Nainital, about 1.4 km long and 463 m wide, ringed by seven hills. Hindu tradition counts it among the Shakti Peeths, where the eye (nain) of the goddess Sati fell. Rowing boats, paddle boats and yachts go out from both ends, and the lake is at its prettiest at night when the hillside lights reflect in it.

North shore of the lake, beside The Flats

The temple of Naina Devi, the goddess of the town, at the upper (Mallital) end of the lake. The old temple was destroyed in the 1880 landslip and a new one built nearby. Its complex hosts the Nanda Devi Mela each September on Nandashtami.

Along the lake, from Mallital to Tallital

The British-built road along the lake that links Mallital and Tallital, officially Govind Ballabh Pant Marg. It is lined with hotels, restaurants, shops and banks; Uttarakhand Tourism suggests handmade candles and pinewood crafts, and an evening stroll for the lake views.

Head of the lake: The Flats, Naina Devi Temple, Bara Bazaar and the ropeway station

The upper, northern end of Naini Lake and the Mall Road. The district site says both ends of the lake have well laid-out shopping centres; Mallital has The Flats, the Naina Devi Temple, the Jama Masjid (1882) and the lower station of the ropeway to Snow View.

Lower, southern end of the lake, at the lake bridge (Danth)

The lower, southern end of the lake. Its bridge, known locally as the Danth, has a statue of Gandhi and a post office the district site calls the only post office on a lake bridge in the world, along with the bus station, taxi stand and a railway reservation counter.

Open ground at the north end of the lake, Mallital

The open ground at Mallital, built on the site of the 1880 landslip, and the town's main meeting place in the evenings. Around it are a statue of Govind Ballabh Pant, a fountain, a bandstand, the Bhotia Market and chaat stalls, the Naina Devi temple, a mosque and a gurudwara, and the Capitol cinema and roller-skating rink.

Hilltop above Mallital, reached by ropeway or road

A hilltop at 2,270 m with views of the snow-covered peaks of the Great Himalaya; a large pair of binoculars is fixed there for a closer look. Most visitors ride the ropeway (cable car) up from Mallital, about 2.5 to 3 minutes each way with views over the town and lake. There are food and tea stalls and a children's amusement park at the top.

Suggested visit
Suggested visit 1-2 h ~ Estimated
Entry
Ropeway, return trip: Rs 300 adult, Rs 200 child up to 12 years (Uttarakhand Tourism, record updated February 2025). The district site gives Rs 190 adult and Rs 120 child (3-12 years), undated. The viewpoint itself has no listed fee. (uttarakhandtourism.gov.in)

Ayarpatta hill, south-west of the lake

A picnic spot and viewpoint at about 2,292 m on Ayarpatta hill, with views over Nainital, the surrounding hills and the Himalaya. Dorothy's Seat is a memorial to the English painter Dorothy Kellet, built by her husband and admirers. Many people walk up; Land's End is next to it and the two are usually done together.

North of the lake, above Snow View

The highest point around Nainital, at about 2,611 m, locally called Cheena Peak. The district site says it commands a view of the snow range from Bandarpunch in the west to Api and Nari in Nepal in the east, with a bird's-eye view of the lake town on the other side. It is reached on foot or by pony, and lies on the Naina Peak-Pangot trek, which starts at Mallital and climbs via Snow View (Uttarakhand Tourism).

Sher Ka Danda hill, above Tallital

A 4.6-hectare high-altitude zoo at about 2,100 m on Sher Ka Danda hill, opened to visitors in 1995. Animals include Himalayan black bear, leopard, tiger, wolves, goral, barking deer and sambar, plus high-altitude pheasants and other birds.

Suggested visit
Suggested visit 1-2 h ~ Estimated
Entry
Indian adult Rs 150, foreign adult Rs 300; children under 12, senior citizens and persons with disabilities free. A combined ticket with the Himalayan Botanical Garden and Woodland Waterfall: Rs 188 adult, Rs 75 child (foreigners Rs 338 / Rs 113). Rates updated June 2026 on the zoo's site. (nainitalzoo.org.in)

Near Sukhatal, Mallital

An Anglican church dedicated to St John the Baptist, built in 1846 and one of the oldest buildings in Nainital. The Neo-Gothic church stands among deodar and pine trees; the hunter-naturalist Jim Corbett was a regular at its services (Uttarakhand Tourism).

Governor's estate, about 4 km by road from the bus station

The Uttarakhand Governor's summer residence, built in 1897-1899 as Government House for the governor of the North-Western Provinces. The Gothic-style building has 113 rooms and the 220-acre estate includes gardens, a swimming pool and a golf course laid out in 1936, where tournaments are still held. Wikipedia now lists it as Lok Bhavan.

Lake town east of Nainital, via Bhowali

A lake town at about 1,370 m whose C-shaped lake, about 47 hectares, is larger than Naini Lake. Boats go out to an aquarium on an island 91 m from the shore, and the 17th-century Bhimeshwar temple stands by the dam. Uttarakhand Tourism also lists the Folk Culture Museum, a butterfly research centre, paragliding and winter birdwatching.

Cluster of lakes in oak forest near Bhimtal

A group of small freshwater lakes at about 1,370 m set in dense oak forest: Nal Damyanti, Panna (Garud) Tal, the Ram, Laxman and Sita lakes, Purna Tal and Sukha Tal. It is a well-known birding spot, with a Methodist ashram founded in 1930 by the missionary Stanley Jones and the Subhash Dhara spring.

4 km beyond Bhimtal

The 'lake of nine corners', at about 1,220 m: 983 m long, 693 m wide and 40.3 m deep, the deepest lake in the Nainital area. It offers rowing, paddling, yachting, angling and birdwatching, with paragliding in the valley and a small Brahma temple by the lake.

Hill village north-east of Nainital, via Bhowali

A hilltop village surrounded by orchards and forest, named after the old Shiva temple (Mukteshwar Dham) on its highest point. Close by are the cliffs of Chauli ki Jaali with views over the Kumaon valleys, and the Indian Veterinary Research Institute campus, founded here by the British in 1893. Uttarakhand Tourism highlights its view of Nanda Devi and other Himalayan peaks.

On the Nainital-Almora road, 9 km past Bhowali

A Hanuman temple and ashram founded by the saint Neem Karoli Baba on the bank of the small Shipra river. It became internationally known through his Western disciples, and later through visits by Steve Jobs and Mark Zuckerberg. The name comes from the two sharp hairpin bends of the road here.
